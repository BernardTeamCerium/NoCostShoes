Diabetic Footwear

How to Choose Diabetic Shoes That Fit Right

November 18, 2026 · 5 min read — What makes a shoe “diabetic,” the features that matter, and why the fitting is as important as the shoe.

If you live with diabetes, your shoes are working harder than most people’s. Reduced circulation and nerve sensation (neuropathy) mean a seam that rubs or a toe box that pinches can turn into a sore you may not feel until it becomes serious. Therapeutic footwear is designed to prevent exactly that.

What Makes a Shoe a Diabetic Shoe

True therapeutic shoes — the kind Medicare covers — are built differently from ordinary comfort shoes:

  • Extra depth throughout the shoe leaves room for custom inserts without crowding your toes.
  • Protective toe boxes are wider and taller, with no interior seams that can rub against skin.
  • Stabilizing soles spread pressure evenly and reduce shock with every step.
  • Adjustable closures — laces, straps, or stretch panels — accommodate swelling that changes through the day.

Brands like Anodyne, Dr. Comfort, Apex, and Orthofeet build entire lines around these requirements, in styles from sport runners to dress shoes and Mary Janes. Looking “medical” is no longer part of the deal — browse our men’s and women’s catalogs and you’ll see modern colors and silhouettes.

Why the Fitting Matters as Much as the Shoe

Medicare requires that therapeutic shoes be fitted by a trained professional — and for good reason. Feet change over time, especially with diabetes. A proper fitting measures both feet for length, width, and depth, checks for pressure points and deformities, and matches you to a style that suits how you actually live. With No Cost Shoes, in-home fittings are available: our fitters come to you.

Signs Your Current Shoes Are Working Against You

  • Red marks or indentations on your feet when you take your shoes off
  • Calluses or corns that keep coming back in the same spots
  • Numbness or tingling that worsens by the end of the day
  • Shoes that feel fine in the morning but tight by evening

If any of these sound familiar, talk to your doctor about therapeutic footwear. With qualifying Medicare or insurance coverage, a pair of doctor-prescribed diabetic shoes and three pairs of custom inserts are covered every calendar year — at little or no cost to you.
