Why proper footwear matters

Small foot problems become big ones fast with diabetes

Diabetes can reduce circulation and feeling in your feet, so blisters, calluses, and pressure points can turn into serious ulcers before you notice them. Therapeutic shoes are designed to prevent that.

  • ✓Extra depth & wide toe boxes remove pressure on bunions, hammertoes, and swelling.
  • ✓Seamless, cushioned interiors protect skin that's vulnerable to blisters and sores.
  • ✓Custom-molded inserts distribute your weight evenly to prevent ulcers.
  • ✓Professional fitting by a certified fitter — required for Medicare coverage, and the key to real comfort.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Are the shoes really free?
If you qualify, Medicare and most insurance plans pay for one pair of diabetic shoes and three pairs of custom-molded inserts per calendar year. Most patients pay nothing beyond any applicable deductible or coinsurance. If you don't qualify, we offer affordable self-pay options starting around $99.
How do I know if I qualify?
You must be diagnosed with diabetes, be under a comprehensive diabetes care plan with your doctor, and have at least one qualifying foot condition — such as neuropathy, poor circulation, foot deformity, calluses, or a history of ulcers. Our short application checks all of this for you in minutes.
Do I need a prescription?
Yes — therapeutic shoes require a prescription from your physician. Don't worry about the paperwork: we coordinate directly with your doctor's office to get the prescription and certification signed.
Who fits my shoes?
Only a trained podiatrist, certified pedorthist, or licensed shoe fitter should fit diabetic shoes — and a professional fitting is required for Medicare or insurance coverage. We make sure you're fitted correctly.
What if my insurance isn't Medicare?
We bill Medicare, Humana, UnitedHealthcare, Aetna, Cigna, Blue Cross Blue Shield, and most other major plans directly. Submit the application and we'll verify your specific benefits at no cost.
