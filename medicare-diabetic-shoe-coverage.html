Medicare Coverage for Diabetic Shoes, Explained

November 12, 2026 · 4 min read — The therapeutic shoe benefit is one of Medicare’s most underused programs. Here’s how it works.

Since 1993, the Therapeutic Shoe Bill has made protective footwear a covered Medicare benefit for people with diabetes who meet certain criteria. Yet many people who qualify never use it — often because the paperwork feels complicated. Here’s the short version.

What Medicare Covers Each Year

For qualifying patients, Medicare Part B covers one pair of extra-depth therapeutic shoes and three pairs of custom inserts every calendar year. Medicare typically pays 80% of the approved amount after your deductible; many supplemental plans pick up the remainder, which is how most patients end up paying little or nothing out of pocket.

Who Qualifies

You may qualify if you have diabetes and at least one of the following, documented by the physician managing your diabetes:

  • Peripheral neuropathy with evidence of callus formation
  • A history of foot ulcers or pre-ulcerative calluses
  • Foot deformity (such as bunions or hammertoes)
  • Poor circulation
  • Previous amputation of a foot or part of a foot

The Steps, Start to Finish

  1. Verification. We check your Medicare or insurance coverage — at no obligation.
  2. Prescription. We coordinate directly with your doctor’s office for the required certification.
  3. Fitting. A trained fitter measures both feet — in-home fittings are available.
  4. Delivery. Your shoes and custom inserts ship to your door, and we handle the billing.

The calendar-year clock resets every January 1, so an unused benefit doesn’t roll over — it’s simply gone. If you haven’t used this year’s benefit, check your eligibility now or read more in our Medicare coverage guide.
