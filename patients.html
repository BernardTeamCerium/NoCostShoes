For patients

Living with diabetes means taking extra care of your health — including safeguarding your feet. The right footwear is one of the most crucial steps in preventing complications, and we're here to guide you on the journey to improved foot health.

Why it matters

Diabetic shoes aren't ordinary shoes

They're specially crafted medical devices designed to prevent the foot complications that diabetes makes dangerous.

Protection from complications

Seamless interiors, protective toe boxes, and cushioned soles guard against the blisters, sores, and ulcers that reduced sensation can hide.

Superior cushioning & support

Extra depth and custom-molded inserts distribute your weight evenly, easing pressure points and keeping you comfortable on your feet all day.

A fit that's actually right

Available in a wide range of sizes and widths — and fitted by a professional, so swelling, bunions, and hammertoes are accounted for.

Breathable materials

Many styles allow air to circulate, supporting a healthier foot environment and reducing moisture-related complications.

Your fitting

What to expect at your professional fitting

Medicare requires that diabetic shoes be fitted by a trained podiatrist, certified pedorthist, or licensed shoe fitter — and it's also how you get shoes that truly fit. Best of all, free in-home fittings are available: our fitters come to you.

  • ✓Both feet measured for length, width, and depth — feet change over time, especially with diabetes.
  • ✓Style selection from athletic, casual, and dress options that match how you actually live.
  • ✓Custom inserts molded to the exact shape of your feet.
  • ✓Fit check and follow-up to make sure everything feels right before you're done.

Daily foot care

Five habits that protect your feet

Good shoes are half the battle. These simple daily habits do the rest.

1. Inspect daily

Check your feet every day for cuts, blisters, redness, or swelling — use a mirror for the soles, or ask a family member to help.

2. Wash & dry gently

Use lukewarm water and mild soap, and dry carefully between the toes where moisture invites infection.

3. Moisturize (not between toes)

Keep skin from cracking with daily lotion on tops and soles — but skip between the toes.

4. Never go barefoot

Even indoors. Reduced sensation means a small object can cause an injury you won't feel.

5. See your doctor promptly

Any sore that doesn't heal within a few days deserves a call to your physician or podiatrist — early treatment prevents serious complications.

And wear your prescribed shoes

Therapeutic shoes only work when they're on your feet. Wear them as your everyday shoes — that's what they're designed for.
